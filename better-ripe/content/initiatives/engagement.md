## 1  Introduction

Recent budget consultations exposed weaknesses in how the RIPE NCC listens to, and speaks with, its community. Criticism intensified after some contributors characterised dissent as a *“loud minority”*—language many found dismissive. The issue escalated when management responses were perceived as defensive, deepening mistrust.

A flash‑point was the Executive Board’s decision on **10 April 2025** to discontinue the *Community Projects Fund (CPF)* for cost‑efficiency reasons, after pausing it in 2024. Community members argued the cut was announced with minimal dialogue and little transparency on alternatives. ([ripe.net](https://www.ripe.net/membership/mail/member-and-community-consultations/draft-activity-plan-and-budget-2025-feedback/))

## 2  Current Situation and Risks

* **Acknowledged problem** – Both staff and volunteers now concede that engagement, especially on contentious topics such as budgets and charging schemes, needs work.
* **Imbalance in engagement modes** – Live events (RIPE Meetings, regional events, Open Houses) dominate. Asynchronous channels (Members‑Discuss, RIPE Forum, working‑group lists) still lack structured facilitation, so nuanced debate is easily lost in long threads or never starts.
* **Reputational risk** – If the pattern repeats—management downplays concerns, the community “moves on” without resolution—the next dispute could trigger a damaging schism.

## 4  Proposed Improvements

| Area                      | Recommendation                                                                                                                                | Benefit                                                        |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Governance**            | Publish an *Engagement Charter* defining response times, escalation paths, and etiquette for both staff and community.                        | Sets clear expectations; reduces “voice not heard” claims.     |
| **Materials**             | Release slide decks and key data **≥ 5 working days** before every Open House or webinar.                                                     | Increases quality of questions; fosters evidence‑based debate. |
| **Asynchronous channels** | Create topic‑specific threads (Members‑Discuss + Forum) immediately after each live event and summarise unanswered questions within *7 days*. | Keeps discussions discoverable; ensures closure.               |
| **Anchors / moderators**  | Nominate *community anchors* per channel—volunteers empowered to summarise threads, flag sentiment, and liaise with NCC staff.                | Bridges gap between informal debate and management decisions.  |
| **Tone management**       | Enforce a simple rule: *criticise ideas, not people*. Immediate moderation of ad‑hominem or dismissive phrases.                               | Lowers temperature; encourages wider participation.            |

## 7  Conclusion

Community engagement is not ancillary to RIPE NCC’s mission—it is the mechanism by which policy legitimacy is maintained. The near‑term fixes (material publication, archiving, moderation) are in place, but the governance‑level items remain open. Implementing the outstanding recommendations on anchors, charter, metrics and audit will convert recent friction into a catalyst for stronger, bidirectional communication and a healthier RIPE ecosystem.
